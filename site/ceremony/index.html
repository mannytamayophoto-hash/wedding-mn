Arrival

Please arrive by 3:30pm, half an hour before the ceremony begins. Intramuros keeps its old cobblestones and uneven stone paving, low, sturdy heels or flats are a kinder choice than anything thin or spiked.

About San Agustin Church

San Agustin traces back to 1571, when the Augustinian friars raised a first church of bamboo and nipa on this ground, twice rebuilt in wood and twice lost to fire. The stone church standing today was begun in 1586 under the architect Juan Macias and finished by 1607, making it the oldest stone church in the Philippines, largely unchanged since it was built.

It is the only structure in Intramuros to survive the bombing of Manila in 1945, and in 1993 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the four Baroque Churches of the Philippines. Inside, a trompe l’oeil ceiling, carved wooden doors, and a High Baroque retablo remain much as they were, and the church still carries its parish name, Immaculate Conception Parish, and its older title, the Archdiocesan Shrine of Our Lady of Consolacion y Correa.

About Intramuros

San Agustin sits inside Intramuros, the walled city the Spanish founded in 1571, governing Manila from within these walls until 1898. Six metre high walls once enclosed about 160 acres in a rough pentagon, moated on the landward side, entered through seven gates, and packed inside with churches, government houses, schools, and the homes of the old Spanish elite.

Most of Intramuros was destroyed in 1945, San Agustin among the very few buildings left standing. What has been rebuilt since, Fort Santiago, Casa Manila, the walls themselves, sits an easy walk from the church, worth the extra hour either side of the ceremony if you have never seen it.
