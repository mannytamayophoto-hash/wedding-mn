frequently asked questions
Are gifts expected?

Your presence at our wedding is present enough. We are travelling for the both of us and simply cannot carry gifts home, so we kindly ask for no physical presents. If you would still like to mark the occasion, a contribution toward our honeymoon or the life we are building together would mean the world, and a Filipino money tree on the day works just as well if that is easier than sending one ahead.

What if I have dietary requirements?

There will be a field for this on the RSVP form once it is live, vegetarian, allergies, anything else, just let us know there and the venue will be told well ahead of the day.

Are kids welcome?

Still being finalised, we will confirm on the RSVP once headcounts are in. If in doubt, ask us directly.

What is the weather going to be like?

Early April is dry season, expect 33 to 34 degrees Celsius and high humidity. Light, breathable fabrics are your friend, the good news is that Filipino formal wear was already built for this climate, a barong and a terno will both sit more comfortably in the heat than a full suit or a heavy gown would. See the Kasuotan page for fabric notes and styling if you are sourcing a barong or Filipiniana.

What if it rains?

San Agustin Church is fully indoors, so the ceremony itself is covered whatever the weather does. April sits in the dry season, so rain on the day is unlikely, but Manila can still surprise you.

Is it safe to visit Manila?

Yes, for the areas you will actually be in. Intramuros, Makati and BGC are well trafficked, tourist familiar, and generally comfortable, ordinary city common sense is all that is needed, watch your bag in crowds, do not flash valuables, use Grab or a hotel arranged taxi rather than hailing one off the street at night. Manila’s emergency number is 911 nationwide, the tourist assistance line is 151-TOUR (8687).

What about food and water?

Do not drink tap water, bottled or filtered water only, including for brushing your teeth if you want to be careful. Restaurant food in the areas covered on the Pasyal page is safe, busy stalls with high turnover are generally fine too if you want to try street food, use the same judgement you would in any big city, if it looks freshly cooked and the stall is busy, it is usually a safe bet.

Anywhere worth avoiding?

Nothing you need to plan around, none of the areas on this site sit anywhere near it, but worth knowing: P. Burgos Street in Makati and parts of the Malate and Ermita districts are Manila’s nightlife and red light strip. Fine to walk through in daylight, just not somewhere to wander into alone late at night looking for a quiet bar, stick to BGC or Poblacion instead for evenings out, see the Pasyal page.

Do I need to speak Tagalog?

Not at all. Every Tagalog phrase on this site carries an English translation beside it, and the same will be true on the day, English is spoken everywhere in Manila.

What about traffic?

Build in more buffer than feels necessary, Manila traffic is genuinely unpredictable and a short trip can take much longer at the wrong time of day. Full detail on the Paglalakbay page, but the short version is: Grab and taxis are easy to find, the road itself is the only variable.

Is there parking at the church or the hotel?

Intramuros has limited street parking around San Agustin, a Grab or taxi is genuinely easier than driving yourself. The Diamond Hotel has its own guest parking for anyone driving to the reception.

Will there be photos to look back on?

Yes, a curated gallery will go up after the wedding. There will also be a shared album link for guests to drop their own candid shots, that link will be added here closer to the date.

By when do I need to RSVP?

15 January 2027. Your household’s unique RSVP code and the form itself will be sent out well ahead of that.

Who do I contact with other questions?

Reach out to either of us directly, or watch the Updates page, anything that comes up often enough gets added here too.
